# Fidelity

What each twin models, how closely, and whether it has been checked against the real service.

**Modeled** means it behaves like the public docs describe. **Approximate** means the shape is right but details differ. **Not yet** means calls fail clearly instead of returning wrong data.

Scenarios can make the field-service twins (Jobber and Housecall Pro) and the books (QuickBooks) disagree on purpose (`world.drift`: respelled customers, customers in only one system, customers the books made inactive who have paid for new work since), the way real businesses' systems drift.

**Verified** means checked against a live vendor sandbox (for Housecall Pro, which has none, a real account), with the date. The [conformance suite](conformance/README.md) does the checking: it records what a real sandbox does for the rows marked "check" and fails a test wherever the twin differs. Nothing has been recorded yet, so everything is built from public documentation: a row says "docs only", or "check" where the docs leave room for doubt, and stays blank when a recording has nothing to settle (a known difference, or something not modeled yet). Anything marked "check" is our best reading of the docs and should be confirmed first.

The twin gives up after 20 attempts (about three days); Intuit doesn't say when it stops. Deliveries go out right after the change, though Intuit says the first "may take up to five minutes". Each URL gets its deliveries one at a time, in order, and different URLs don't wait for each other. Intuit may hold back later events until a failing one is answered, and notifies only apps connected to the company by OAuth; the twin does neither |
